TL;DR: If Google is putting inline links higher in AI answers, publishers should optimize for being cited inside the answer, not just ranking below it.

## What did Google reportedly change?

Search Engine Journal’s Matt G. Southern reported in “Google Puts Inline Links Higher In More AI Search Responses” that Google is changing how links appear in AI Mode and AI Overviews. The reported change has two parts: inline links are moving higher in more AI search responses, and Google is expanding carousels that highlight Preferred Sources.

That sounds small. It is not.

The biggest complaint about AI answers in search has been simple: Google gets the answer, publishers get the cost. If citations are buried, collapsed, or visually detached from the generated answer, the web becomes background material. Users may never click. Publishers still fund the reporting, product pages, documentation, recipes, reviews, and local information that make the answer possible.

Putting inline links higher does not solve that whole problem. It may not even come close. But placement matters. A link inside or near the answer is different from a link after the answer. It changes what the user sees as part of the response, not just supporting paperwork.

The catch: this is trade reporting, not a first-party Google announcement included in the material here. So I would treat the mechanics as reported, not fully specified. We do not have rollout timing, eligibility rules, click impact, query coverage, or a clean before-and-after dataset from Google in the supplied source.

## Does this help publishers, or just make AI search look more fair?

Both can be true.

Inline links higher in the response may send more visible credit to sources. It may also make AI Overviews and AI Mode feel less like closed answers and more like guided search. That is good for user trust. It is also good for Google, because visible citations reduce the feeling that the answer appeared from nowhere.

For publishers, the real question is not “are links present?” It is “do the links create meaningful visits?” A citation that gets seen but not clicked has branding value, maybe. A citation that gets clicked by someone with intent has business value. Those are different.

Preferred Sources carousels are also worth watching. If Google gives users or systems more ways to surface selected sources, authority and habit may matter more. Big known brands could benefit. Niche publishers with strong topical focus could also benefit, if Google’s source selection actually rewards depth and consistency instead of just fame.

I run Lucky Domains, which does [SEO and search visibility work](https://luckydomains.io/services.html#seo), so my bias is practical: AI search visibility only matters if it connects to pages that answer the next question better than the AI box can.

That means the old SEO reflex, publish more pages around more keywords, looks weaker here. The better play is to become the kind of source a model can safely cite: clear claims, named entities, dated pages, original evidence, tight structure, and content that resolves a specific user task.

## What should site owners change now?

Do not rebuild your strategy around one reported UI change. Do use it as a prompt to audit whether your pages are citation-ready.

Start with the pages that already rank, convert, or earn links. Ask whether a generated answer could quote or cite them cleanly. If the page buries the answer under a long intro, weakens the claim with fluff, or mixes five search intents into one asset, it is harder to use as a source.

Then look at query shape. AI Overviews and AI Mode are strongest where the user asks for synthesis: comparisons, definitions, planning, troubleshooting, product selection, and “what should I do?” pages. Those pages need to be more than keyword containers. They need specific judgment, constraints, examples, and evidence.

Also separate source value from landing page value. A page may be great for citation because it defines a concept cleanly. Another may be great for conversion because it helps someone choose a vendor, tool, or next step. Sometimes one page can do both. Often it cannot.

For measurement, watch Search Console patterns, but do not expect perfect attribution. AI answer clicks may blur with normal search traffic, and visibility without clicks may still influence branded search later. Track the boring signals too: assisted conversions, branded queries, direct visits to cited resources, and changes on pages most likely to be referenced.

The practitioner’s move is simple: pick 10 important pages and rewrite them for answer extraction plus human follow-through. Put the direct answer near the top, support it with specifics, make the source of each claim obvious, and give the visitor a reason to continue after the AI summary. The catch most teams miss is that citation visibility is not the finish line. It is the handoff.
