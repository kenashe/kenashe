TL;DR: If AI Overview visibility is being flattened into Search Console position data, ranking reports can look better while the business outcome gets worse, so measure search by qualified traffic and conversions.

## What is block flattening doing to AI Overview reporting?

Search Engine Journal’s Taylor DanRW reported in “Search Console Uses Block Flattening For AIOs, Forget Position & Focus On Outcomes” that Google Search Console can treat AI Overview results in a way that makes weak visibility look like a strong organic position.

The key idea is block flattening. Instead of each link inside a search feature getting its own visible slot, the whole block can be treated as occupying one position. If an AI Overview appears near the top of the page, a cited page buried inside that AI block may inherit a flattering position signal.

That creates a reporting problem. A page can appear to “rank” near the top, while the actual user experience is much weaker. The citation may be tucked inside an expandable section, surrounded by other cited sources, or simply not prominent enough to earn a click.

This is not a small analytics quirk. It changes how teams read performance. A content manager sees position improving. An executive sees SEO “working.” But traffic is flat, leads are down, and nobody can explain the mismatch without understanding how SERP features distort the old rank model.

## Why average position is now a worse scoreboard

Average position was always a compressed metric. It hid query mix, device differences, geography, personalization, and SERP layout. AI Overviews make the compression more painful.

A blue link at position two is not the same as a citation inside an AI-generated answer that Search Console may report as position two. One is a direct invitation to click. The other is often supporting material for an answer Google already gave on the page.

That does not mean AI Overview citations are worthless. They can still create brand visibility. They can validate authority. They may send traffic in some cases. But the metric needs to match the behavior. If users get enough information from the overview, the citation may produce impressions without visits. If the query has commercial intent, the overview may change which pages get explored next.

This is where I think SEO reporting has to grow up. Rank tracking still has diagnostic value, but it is no longer the boardroom number. The better question is not “Did we win position one?” It is “Did this search surface produce the next useful action?”

Ashe runs [Lucky Domains, which works on SEO and search visibility](https://luckydomains.io/services.html#seo), and this is exactly the kind of reporting shift that matters when a site has to rank and produce business value, not just screenshots.

## What should teams measure instead?

Start with Search Console, but do not stop there. Treat impressions and average position as directional inputs, not proof of success. Then connect them to landing page sessions, engaged visits, assisted conversions, form fills, calls, trials, demos, purchases, or whatever the site actually exists to create.

For AI Overview-heavy queries, segment branded and non-branded searches. Separate informational queries from commercial ones. Look for pages where impressions rise while clicks fall. That pattern may mean the content is being used to answer the query without earning the visit.

Also watch the query itself. If a page ranks for broad “what is” searches, AI answers may absorb more of the demand. If the page answers a task with depth, tools, comparisons, pricing context, local details, or first-hand experience, it has a better shot at earning the click after the summary.

The catch is that teams want one clean replacement metric. There probably is not one. AI search breaks the fantasy that rank equals attention equals revenue. The practical move is to build a small dashboard that ties query visibility to page-level outcomes, then review the mismatches every month. If position improves but qualified traffic does not, rewrite the goal before rewriting the page.
