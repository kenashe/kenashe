## The capability gap looks small from the outside

GPT-5.6 Sol is being framed as OpenAI’s answer to Anthropic’s Fable and Mythos line. Fine. The more useful read is less dramatic: the frontier labs are still tracking each other closely.

On IBM’s Mixture of Experts, Kush Varshney said the models look like a back-and-forth on capability, with the interesting divergence showing up in safety design. Lauren McHugh Olende made a similar point from another angle: she did not see a breakthrough as large as last year’s jump around chain-of-thought style reasoning. Sol’s scientific reasoning claims caught her eye, but the example she cited, 7% accuracy on a protein or molecule binding task, is both impressive and humbling. That is a hard domain. It is also not “replace the lab” territory.

AI Explained’s read was even more cautious on hard comparisons. Sol is available only to select customers, and its report card is incomplete. That matters. When the public data is thin, the honest answer is not “Sol wins” or “Fable wins.” It is: we do not have enough to know, and the differences users feel may come from everything around the model.

## The safety layer is becoming the user experience

The most interesting split is not personality or branding. It is where the labs put the guardrails.

Varshney described Sol as closer to defense-in-depth: extra safety training inside the model, external guardrails, and a reasoning-style checker that can analyze responses before they go out. That is different from the picture painted of Anthropic’s Fable setup, where Mythos is described as a more naked model and Fable as relying heavily on a classifier layer.

That flips the stereotype a bit. Anthropic is the company people associate with safety-first posture. OpenAI, at least in this Sol release, appears to be showing more visible layers. McHugh Olende also reminded the panel that OpenAI’s original public identity in 2019 was basically safety, so maybe this is less a reversal than a return.

The catch is that safety is not abstract for users. AI Explained said Anthropic’s improved classifier, added after an Amazon-flagged vulnerability, may block benign requests more often, including routine coding and debugging. Anthropic also said no universal jailbreak had been found at the time, though red teaming continues. Both things can be true: the model can be harder to abuse and more annoying to use.

For builders, that tradeoff is not philosophical. It is tickets, retries, fallbacks, and angry users who were trying to debug normal code.

## Price may decide what benchmarks cannot

Pricing is the other practical signal. AI Explained said GPT-5.6 Sol is priced at exactly half Fable 5 on input and just over half on output. If Sol is roughly comparable for common workloads, that is not a footnote. That changes routing decisions, margins, and what teams can afford to run in production.

This is also where Wall Street’s AI nerves make sense. The labs are spending huge amounts to reach models that, from the outside, often look close enough that buyers start comparing restrictions, latency, and cost per task. If every new flagship is “better, but not obviously transformative,” the product wrapper becomes the battlefield.

That does not mean benchmarks are useless. It means benchmark deltas are not the whole purchase decision anymore. A model that scores slightly higher but blocks 3% of your normal workflows is worse for that app. A cheaper model that needs two retries may not be cheaper. A safer model that cannot explain why it refused can quietly destroy trust.

Practitioner’s take: do not pick Sol, Fable, Sonnet, or anything else from release charts alone. Build a small eval set from your real workload: normal coding tasks, edge-case user prompts, domain questions, refusal-prone prompts, and cost-sensitive long-context jobs. Log answer quality, refusal rate, retry rate, latency, and total cost. The catch most teams miss is that safety layers change over time, sometimes without your app changing at all. Treat model selection as an operating process, not a launch-day bet.
