TL;DR: AI search makes your website less like a brochure and more like source material, so the job is to make facts, proof, and next steps easy for machines and humans to trust.

## What job does the website have when AI answers first?

The useful primary source here is Search Engine Journal’s “AI Search Is Changing Your Website’s Job, Here’s How To Adapt,” where Loren Baker covers Sachin Puri’s argument that AI discovery raises the value of accurate business information, trust signals, and measurement beyond clicks.

That framing is right. The website is not dead. It is being demoted from the only destination to one of several inputs.

For a small business, that changes the work. The old mental model was simple: rank the page, win the click, convert the visitor. The newer model is messier. A customer might ask Google, ChatGPT, Perplexity, or another assistant for a recommendation. The assistant may pull from your site, business profiles, reviews, third-party directories, local listings, social content, product pages, and old cached facts. Sometimes the click still happens. Sometimes your business gets considered without a visit. Sometimes you lose because the machine found stale hours, fuzzy service areas, or thin proof.

That is not an argument to abandon SEO. It is an argument to stop treating the website as a sealed container. AI search rewards consistency across the open web, and it punishes ambiguity.

I run Lucky Domains, which works on SEO and search visibility at and the operational pattern I see is boring but important: most businesses do not have an AI search problem first. They have a facts problem, a trust problem, or a measurement problem.

## What should change on the site itself?

Start with the facts. Not slogans. Facts.

Who you serve. Where you serve. What you sell. What it costs, if pricing can be stated. What the process looks like. What makes you qualified. What someone should do next. If a human has to infer the basics from a hero section and three vague icons, an AI system will not magically understand it better.

The best pages for AI search are often the best pages for impatient buyers. Clear service pages. Specific location pages, when the business is genuinely local. Useful FAQ sections that answer real objections. Case studies with enough detail to prove the work happened. Author, team, policy, and contact pages that make the business feel accountable.

Structured data helps, but it is not a substitute for clarity. Same with schema, citations, and metadata. Those are packaging layers. If the underlying page says “solutions for modern teams” instead of “commercial HVAC repair for restaurants in Phoenix,” no markup will save it.

Puri’s point about customer trust matters because AI search compresses comparison. When an assistant summarizes options, weak proof gets shaved off. Reviews, testimonials, photos, product specs, refund policies, certifications, delivery areas, and support details all become part of the evidence layer.

## What should you measure if clicks are less complete?

Clicks still matter. They are just no longer the whole scoreboard.

If AI search sends fewer visits but better prepared buyers, a raw traffic chart can make the business look worse while the pipeline improves. The reverse can also happen. AI summaries might mention you, but send no qualified demand. So the measurement stack has to widen.

Track branded search volume directionally. Track calls, forms, bookings, map actions, email inquiries, and assisted conversions. Ask new customers how they found you, even if the answer is imperfect. Watch referral patterns from AI products where visible. Monitor how your business is described in answer engines, especially for high-intent prompts.

Do not obsess over one screenshot from one AI answer. These systems vary by user, location, phrasing, and time. The useful question is whether your public information gives them enough accurate material to represent you well.

Practically, I would pick five customer questions and test them across Google, ChatGPT, and Perplexity. Then compare the answers against your actual website and business profiles. Fix every stale fact. Add one page that answers a buying question better than your competitors do. Add proof where claims are naked. The catch most readers miss: AI search adaptation is not a plugin. It is information hygiene, trust building, and better pages, repeated.
