TL;DR: SEO teams need to measure AI search visibility separately from classic rankings, because the click is no longer the only useful signal.

## What actually changed for SEO teams?

Matt Southern’s Search Engine Journal piece, “Core Update, ChatGPT Business Hub & AI Mode’s New Model – SEO Pulse,” bundles the right set of signals: a Google core update, AI Mode getting a new default model, ChatGPT sharing mention and click counts, and publishers planning for less search traffic.

That mix matters more than any single item.

Classic SEO has always been a moving target. Core updates shift rankings. SERP layouts change. Featured snippets compress clicks. None of that is new. What is new is that AI search creates another layer between user intent and the site visit. A model may mention you. It may summarize you. It may cite you. It may send a click. Or it may satisfy the user without sending anything at all.

That makes the old scoreboard too narrow.

Rank tracking still has value. Search Console still has value. Logs still have value. But if a brand is appearing inside AI-generated answers and not seeing the visit, the team needs a way to record that exposure. If a brand is not appearing at all, the team needs to know that too. Not as vanity. As distribution.

I run Lucky Domains, where SEO and search visibility are part of the work, so I care less about the drama around “SEO is dead” and more about which pages keep getting discovered when the interface changes: 

## Are mentions the new clicks?

Not exactly.

A mention is weaker than a click. It does not prove the user read deeply, trusted the source, or took action. It can also be hard to audit without clear reporting. Southern reported that ChatGPT is sharing mention and click counts, which is directionally important, but the details need to come from OpenAI’s own business docs or announcements before anyone treats the mechanics as settled.

Still, mentions are not nothing.

In AI search, the first funnel step may be “included in the answer,” not “ranked number three.” That changes how I would build reporting. I would not replace traffic reports with AI visibility reports. I would add a layer above them.

The basic question becomes: where does the brand show up when a user asks the assistant the questions that used to produce high-intent search traffic?

That includes product comparisons, local service questions, “best provider for X” queries, troubleshooting searches, definitions, buying guides, and post-purchase support. Some of those will still drive clicks. Some will only drive memory. Some will produce bad summaries. All three are worth knowing.

## What should builders change now?

The practical shift is from keyword-only SEO to answer-surface strategy.

That does not mean stuffing pages with FAQ blocks and hoping an AI system quotes them. It means publishing pages that are easy to identify, easy to trust, and easy to extract from. Clear authorship. Specific claims. Fresh dates where freshness matters. Direct comparisons. Real examples. Pages that answer one job well instead of vaguely covering a topic cloud.

It also means separating three reports that often get blurred together: rankings, AI visibility, and traffic. Rankings tell you how classic search treats you. AI visibility tells you whether assistants can find and represent you. Traffic tells you whether any of it produced a session.

Those are different instruments.

Core updates still matter because Google traffic still matters. AI Mode matters because Google is changing the search experience itself. ChatGPT business reporting matters because assistant visibility may become a boardroom metric faster than SEOs expect. But none of this justifies panic. It just means the measurement stack is behind the interface.

Practitioner’s take: pick 20 high-intent queries that matter to your business, test them across Google, AI Mode if you have access, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and any vertical assistant your customers might use, then log whether you are mentioned, cited, accurately described, and clicked when reporting exists. The catch most teams miss is that the fix is usually not “write more AI content.” It is tightening the pages that already should be the canonical answer.
