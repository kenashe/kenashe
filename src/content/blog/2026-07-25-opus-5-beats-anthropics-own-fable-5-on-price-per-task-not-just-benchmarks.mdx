TL;DR: Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 lands at about half the price of its own flagship Fable 5 while matching or beating it on most benchmarks, which means the story isn't raw intelligence anymore, it's cost per completed task.

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 this week, and the framing from the company is strange enough to notice: they say Opus 5 is *not* more capable than their most general model, Claude Fable 5. Then you look at the benchmarks and Opus 5 is out front on most of them. The AI Grid put the question bluntly in their title, "are they lying?" The answer is more interesting than a yes or no, and it comes down to what "capable" means when one model costs half as much as the other.

I'm working from three tier-2 walkthroughs here: Matthew Berman's "What did Anthropic do?! (Opus 5)," The AI Grid's breakdown, and Alex Finn's hands-on tests. None of them is a primary source in the strict sense, and I'll flag where the numbers are thin. But across all three, the same shape shows up, and that shape is the actual news.

## Why does Opus 5 beat Fable 5 if Anthropic says it's not more capable?

Because "capable" and "worth using" have quietly separated.

On Anthropic's own published charts, Opus 5 leads Fable 5 on agentic terminal coding, Frontier Bench (43 vs 33 per Berman), GDPval (a roughly 100-point jump), ARC-AGI-3 (around 30%), and BrowseComp (90% vs 87%). Humanity's Last Exam is basically a tie with tools. OSWorld, the computer-use benchmark, shows a four-point improvement. So on the workloads people actually run, agents, computer use, coding, Opus 5 is either ahead or even.

![two ascending paths starting from the same point, one rising higher while curving toward the cheaper side of a plane](../../assets/blog/2026-07-25-opus-5-beats-anthropics-own-fable-5-on-price-per-task-not-just-benchmarks/inline-1.png)

The losses are real too, and worth naming because the hype videos skate past them. Berman flagged a drop on the legal benchmark (13.3 down to 11.7) and a decrease on HealthBench Professional. DeepSWE, which he calls the best benchmark, ticked down slightly. So if your work is legal reasoning or clinical, Fable 5 may still edge it. Anthropic's "not more capable" line is technically defensible: on a few specialized frontier tasks, Fable 5 holds.

But here's the thing. When one model costs half as much and wins on most of the workloads people run daily, the specialized wins stop mattering for most teams. That's why The AI Grid kept repeating "it doesn't really make sense to use Fable 5." The gap where Fable leads is narrow, and you pay double to sit in it.

## What is cost per task, and why does it matter more than price per token?

This is the part I want operators to internalize, because it's the real lesson buried under the "DESTROYS Fable 5" thumbnails.

Berman made the sharpest point across all three videos: price per token is a trap. His example was Kimi K3, which came in at less than half the price per token of GPT-5.6 Soul and Fable 5, yet used roughly twice as many tokens to finish the same task. Net result: a wash. You pay the same. The cheap-per-token model wasn't cheap at all once it actually did the work.

So the metric that matters is cost per completed task: how much money it takes to get from prompt to a correct, finished output. That folds in token efficiency, pass rate, and retries, all the things a per-token sticker price hides.

Anthropic leaned into this framing hard, which is smart and also self-serving. On the OSWorld chart Berman described, Opus 5 sits higher (better score) *and* further left (cheaper) than Fable 5 and Opus 4.8. GPT-5.6 Soul spans a huge price range, and to match Opus 5's performance it reportedly costs over twice as much even at Opus 5's lowest setting. Treat that specific "twice as much" number as Anthropic's own chart, not independent verification. But the general shape, better output at lower spend, is the pitch, and it's a pitch built on the right axis.

## Do the independent benchmarks back up Anthropic's claims?

Partly, and this is where The AI Grid did the most useful work of the three.

Anthropic's own charts carry obvious incentive to look good. So The AI Grid pulled third-party indexes. On the Apex Agents benchmark from Mercor, which tests investment banking associates, management consultants, big-law associates, and primary-care physicians against 200-plus real tasks with expert rubrics, Opus 5 leads at 43.5%. Marginally ahead of Fable 5, but again: half the price. On Apex SWE, built with Cognition to test integration and observability, Fable 5 leads slightly, and the honest read is that the gap is small enough that pricing decides it. There's also the Vals index, which weights model performance by economic sector proportional to US GDP, though the specific Opus 5 result there wasn't cited.

I'd caution against treating any of these as settled. These are creator walkthroughs, benchmark names are getting garbled across videos (Fable, Soul, DeepSWE, none are standard public releases I can independently confirm), and a lot of the "100-point improvement" language is impressive-sounding without a clear denominator. What's consistent across all three sources is the direction: Opus 5 matches or beats Fable on most real-work benchmarks at half the cost.

## Where does Opus 5 actually fall short?

Alex Finn was the only one to hands-on it, and he was the most useful because he tried to break it.

His homemade five-test suite (a 3D roller-coaster simulator, an Apple-website clone, and three others) had Opus 5 beating Fable 5 on output quality and cost. The roller coaster came in at 82 cents and 32,000 tokens on Opus 5, versus roughly 50% more expensive on Fable 5 for a rougher result. Fable actually used fewer tokens on that task but cost more, which is exactly the per-token-vs-per-task point in miniature.

Finn also teased "three insane weaknesses" and "three major deal breakers," then the transcript cuts off before he lists them. So I can't tell you what they are, and I won't invent them. That's a real gap in the coverage: the most interesting claim in his video, the downsides, is the part we don't have. Treat "no reason to use Fable 5 anymore" as an enthusiastic take, not a verdict, until those weaknesses are on the table.

## Practitioner's take

If you run anything on Claude, re-run your cost math this week, and run it per task, not per token. Take five representative jobs from your actual workload, agent runs, coding tasks, document pipelines, and measure total spend to a correct finish on Opus 5 versus whatever you're on now. Include retries and failed attempts, because that's where token-cheap models quietly bleed you. The likely outcome, based on what all three creators saw, is that Opus 5 does the same work for roughly half of Fable 5's bill, which makes it the default for general and agentic work.

The catch most readers will miss: don't switch *everything*. The benchmark drops on legal and health tasks are real, and Anthropic's own "not more capable" hedge is pointing at exactly those specialized frontiers. If your domain is one of them, test both and let your own eval decide, not a thumbnail that says one model destroys the other. The honest headline isn't "Opus 5 wins." It's "the price of doing the same work just dropped by half, so measure the work, not the token."
