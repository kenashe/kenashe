TL;DR: The useful enterprise AI pattern is not “give everyone ChatGPT,” it is turning messy internal notes and role-specific needs into repeatable training packages people can actually use.

## What did RealPage say it is doing with ChatGPT Work?

OpenAI’s customer video, **“From notes to AI training with ChatGPT Work | Realpage.”**, centers on Austin Bram, director of AI enablement at RealPage. Bram says his job is helping team members understand how to use AI in practical ways that improve day-to-day work.

That sounds obvious until you sit inside a company trying to do it.

Different teams need different examples. Legal does not need the same training as customer support. A beginner needs different scaffolding than someone already building workflows. The hard part is not producing one impressive demo. The hard part is keeping education relevant, consistent, and updated without turning the AI enablement team into a slide factory.

Bram says he uses ChatGPT Work to take his ideas, notes, and source materials and turn them into “complete enablement packages.” He also says this helps him provide more relevant and consistent AI education so RealPage team members can use AI more effectively and create better outcomes for customers and the business.

That is the claim. Not a benchmark. Not a deployment architecture. A workflow claim from an operator.

And it is worth taking seriously for exactly that reason.

## Why does this matter more than another prompt library?

Prompt libraries age fast. They get copied into docs, ignored in Slack, and rediscovered three quarters later by someone who cannot tell whether they still match company policy.

Training packages are different if they are built from current internal material. A good package can include the role context, the task pattern, the safe and unsafe examples, the review step, and the expected output quality. It can teach judgment, not just syntax.

That is where I think the OpenAI and RealPage example points. The model is being used less as a magic answer box and more as an internal curriculum production system.

![messy notes and internal documents flowing into structured training kits for several different employee groups](../../assets/blog/2026-10-24-ai-enablement-is-becoming-curriculum-ops/inline-1.webp)

The catch is that consistency can be fake. If an AI system turns weak notes into polished training, the polish can hide gaps. If the source material is stale, the training is stale. If the internal policy is vague, the lesson will be vague with nicer formatting.

So the useful operating question is not “Can ChatGPT generate training?” Of course it can generate training-shaped material.

The better question is: who owns the source material, who checks the examples, and how often does the package get refreshed?

## What should enterprise AI teams copy from this?

Copy the shape of the workflow, not the testimonial.

Start with one narrow job family and one recurring task. For example, account managers summarizing customer calls, analysts drafting first-pass reports, or support teams rewriting responses for tone and accuracy. Collect the actual messy inputs: notes, examples, policies, past outputs, review comments. Then use the AI system to draft the training package from those materials.

But keep humans in the loop where they matter. A subject-matter expert should check the examples. A manager should confirm the task reflects the real job. Someone responsible for risk should flag what not to do. The AI can compress the production cycle, but it should not become the final authority on what “good” looks like inside the company.

I would also measure boring things. How long did it take to create the package? How many people completed it? Did their outputs improve under manager review? Did support tickets, revision cycles, or escalation rates change? If you cannot see behavior change, you may just have prettier enablement collateral.

The move I’d make: pick one team, build one AI training package from real internal artifacts, run it with 10 to 20 users, and compare their work before and after. The catch most readers miss is governance. The fastest way to scale AI education is also the fastest way to scale outdated assumptions, so make refresh and review part of the workflow from day one.
