TL;DR: If you want AI systems to mention your brand, build a consistent entity footprint across credible places, because one-off PR hits are weaker than repeated, structured evidence.

## What changes when PR is judged by AI systems?

Marketing AI Institute’s “Why Entity-Led Digital PR Is the New Path to AI Visibility” makes a useful distinction: traditional PR still matters, but it is no longer enough if the goal is showing up in ChatGPT, Gemini, or Google’s AI Overviews when someone asks for a recommendation.

That feels right. The old playbook optimized for human attention. Pitch a journalist. Win a mention. Get traffic, links, awareness, maybe a sales bump. The newer problem is different. AI systems need to understand what your company is, what category it belongs in, who trusts it, and whether that pattern appears in enough credible places to be worth repeating.

That is an entity problem.

An entity is not just a name. It is the bundle of signals around that name: the company, founders, products, services, locations, categories, awards, reviews, mentions, profiles, schema, citations, and relationships to other known things. If those signals are scattered, thin, or inconsistent, a model or search system has less to work with.

This is where a lot of AI visibility advice gets sloppy. People want a trick. Add a page. Mention ChatGPT. Publish an “AI-friendly” FAQ. Fine, maybe useful at the margin. But the deeper work is boring and compound: make the brand legible across the web.

I run Lucky Domains, where part of the work is helping businesses improve [SEO and search visibility](https://luckydomains.io/services.html#seo), and this maps to what I see in practice: machines reward clarity before cleverness.

## Is this just digital PR with a new label?

Partly. There is some renaming happening. PR people have always cared about credibility, third-party mentions, and association with known publications or experts. SEO people have always cared about links, citations, topical authority, and structured signals. Entity-led PR sits in the overlap.

The useful part is the operating model.

Instead of asking, “Can we get coverage?” the better question is, “What should the web believe this brand is known for, and where would a machine expect to find proof?”

That changes the targets. A random mention on a big site may be less useful than a precise mention in the right category context. A founder quote may matter more if it ties the person to a consistent area of expertise. A directory listing may be boring, but if it confirms the same company name, category, address, and service set, it can help reduce ambiguity.

The risk is cargo-cult PR. Teams will create entity diagrams, stuff pages with schema, and call it strategy. That misses the point. Entity signals only help if they reflect something real. If your product is vague, your positioning changes every quarter, and your third-party footprint is mostly paid fluff, AI systems are not the only audience that will be confused.

Marketing AI Institute is directionally right here, but the hard part is measurement. We still do not have a clean, universal dashboard for “AI visibility.” ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and other systems behave differently. Their outputs shift. Personalization, retrieval, query phrasing, and geography can all matter. Anyone selling a guaranteed placement in AI answers is selling certainty they do not have.

## What should a builder actually do?

Start with the entity audit. Search the brand, founders, product names, and category terms. Then ask major AI assistants the same questions a buyer would ask. Not vanity prompts. Real prompts: “Who are the best providers for X?” “What companies help with Y?” “Is Z a credible company?” Capture what appears, what is missing, and what is wrong.

Then fix the basics. Make the website unambiguous. Use consistent naming. Explain the category in plain language. Connect people to the company. Keep profiles current. Add schema where it matches the page. Clean up conflicting listings. Build pages that answer actual category questions, not just branded slogans.

After that, earn better corroboration. Guest quotes, niche publications, partner pages, podcasts, conference bios, customer stories, review platforms, and analyst mentions can all help if they reinforce the same story. The point is not volume for volume’s sake. It is repeated evidence from places a search or AI system can reasonably treat as context.

The practitioner move this week: pick one money category you want to be associated with, then map every public signal that supports or contradicts that association. Fix contradictions first. Then build three credible third-party proofs around that category. The catch most teams miss: AI visibility is not a campaign. It is the byproduct of being consistently identifiable.
